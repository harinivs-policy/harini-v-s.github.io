Southern Demographic Resilience Pilot

Can Southern India turn demographic change into economic resilience?

A proposed five-year pilot across five southern states, designed to address the intersection of population ageing, local workforce constraints and growing labour demand.

5 States 10 Proposed Districts 5-Year Proposal Year 1: Preparation

Demographic change is not only a challenge to manage. It is an economic transition to prepare for.

01 / THE CONTEXT

A changing demographic landscape

Southern states are ageing faster than India as a whole. At the same time, local workforce growth is slowing while demand for labour continues to rise.

02 / THE POLICY PROBLEM

Three pressures. One connected challenge.

The triple pressure

  • A growing share of the population entering older age groups.
  • Slow growth of the native workforce.
  • Rising labour demand across the regional economy.

The emerging gap

Local youth may remain unemployed even as employers depend on migrant workers. Migrants can arrive without secure contracts, adequate housing or portable welfare access.

The resulting pressures include urban congestion, informal exploitation and wage anxiety among local workers.

03 / THE PROPOSED RESPONSE

Four instruments. One regional approach.

01

Increase local labour productivity

Place women at the centre through industry-linked apprenticeships, safe transport, return-to-work schemes and childcare.

02

Establish one fair labour standard

Led by the Ministry of Labour and Employment, with common minimum wages, skill recognition, contracts and safety rules for local and migrant workers. A portable worker account would build on e-Shram and One Nation One Ration Card.

03

Make dignified ageing an economic opportunity

Develop trained and certified carers with social protection.

04

Create a Destination District Workforce Fund

A proposed Union–state–employer funding mechanism to strengthen services in districts with high migrant loads.

Five states. Ten districts.

05 / YEAR 1

From approval to a costed implementation plan

Year 1 is a preparation phase covering baseline surveys, district selection, costing and target-setting. It requires no new funding.

By Month 3

Consolidate baseline evidence

The Ministry consolidates existing migrant survey data for Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Kerala, and commissions surveys for Telangana and Andhra Pradesh.

By Month 6

Select districts and establish costs

NITI Aayog, the Ministry and the five state governments jointly select ten districts, cost the pilot and determine the proposed Union–state–employer funding split. Existing secondary data would be used for Telangana and Andhra Pradesh until surveys report.

By October 2027 · End of Year 1

Approve the costed Years 2–5 plan

NITI Aayog and the Ministry set targets and submit the costed plan for approval by the Vice-Chairperson.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION CONSIDERATIONS

The trade-offs cannot be ignored

Short-term costs

Formalisation, housing and welfare services raise costs for states and employers.

Local perceptions

Residents may see the pilot as favouring migrants, although local skilling and protected care jobs directly benefit residents.

Employer compliance

Some employers may evade labour rules, particularly in informal settings.

Time to impact

Enforcement and institutional change may take years to reach informal employers.

The SIIMS 2026 survey cited in the note is indicative, not representative: it surveyed 1,824 migrant workers in Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka. The note reports that 19.4% could easily access welfare after migrating, while 45.3% faced difficulty.

07 / THE DECISION SOUGHT

Approve Year 1 of the proposed pilot.

The recommendation is for NITI Aayog to approve a 12-month preparation phase beginning upon approval, with the Ministry of Labour and Employment leading the process.

The immediate decision is to establish the evidence, select the districts, determine costs and set targets before seeking approval for Years 2–5.
