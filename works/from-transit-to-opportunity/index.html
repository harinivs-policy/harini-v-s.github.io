Urban Mobility · Employment Access

From Transit
to Opportunity

Reimagining public transport as a pathway to employment through Employment-Oriented Transit Development (E-TOD).

Transport infrastructure is only as effective as the opportunities it connects people to. This project examines the gap between urban transit networks and employment destinations, proposing a policy framework that places workers' access to jobs at the centre of transport-oriented development.

Policy Impact Challenge Series · PICS II Cohort Challenge 6 + Challenge 7 Policy Proposal & Implementation Framework
Challenge 6 · Policy Brief
01 / The policy problem

When transport expands, do employment opportunities become more accessible?

Cities may invest in metro lines, roads and transit infrastructure while workers continue to face difficult, expensive and time-consuming journeys to their places of work.

Employment centres, industrial clusters and emerging job hubs are often located beyond convenient public transport connections. As a result, commuters may depend on multiple modes of transport to complete a single journey.

These gaps can increase travel costs, extend commute times and deepen inequalities in access to employment. They can also contribute to private vehicle dependence, congestion and environmental pressure.

The burden is not experienced equally. Workers with limited transport choices, including many women and lower-income commuters, can face additional barriers to reaching work.

"A transport network should not merely move people across a city. It should connect them to opportunity."
02 / The proposed policy response

From Development-Oriented Transit to Employment-Oriented Transit

Employment-Oriented Transit Development (E-TOD) shifts the focus from infrastructure-led urban expansion to the accessibility of jobs, workers and employment-generating destinations.

01 / ACCESS MEASUREMENT

Public Jobs Access Score

Introduce a city-level measure to assess how effectively public transport connects residents to employment opportunities.

02 / AFFORDABILITY

Worker Transit Pass

Develop a shared-cost transit pass involving employers, workers and government to reduce commuting expenditure.

03 / HOUSING

Affordable Housing Near Transit

Introduce affordable housing provisions around transit stations to bring workers closer to employment and mobility networks.

04 / LAND USE

Employment-Generating Clusters

Encourage office, industrial and employment-generating activities near transit corridors instead of concentrating development only around retail and residential uses.

05 / FINANCING

Blended Funding Mechanisms

Explore viability gap funding, value capture finance and co-funding arrangements to support implementation.

06 / INTEGRATION

Mode-Neutral Connectivity

Integrate buses, feeder services, e-autos and metro systems into a connected public transport network.

07 / LOCAL CONTEXT

Context-Sensitive Planning

Adapt E-TOD approaches to existing job centres, emerging growth corridors and the needs of smaller cities.

08 / INCLUSION

Universal Accessibility

Make accessibility, affordability and inclusive mobility central considerations in transit planning and delivery.

45 minutes
Proposed target: enable workers to reach employment destinations within 45 minutes.
10% or less
Proposed target: keep daily commuting expenditure at or below 10% of a worker's daily wage.

Challenge 7 · Implementation & Impact
04 / Translating policy into action

From policy vision to implementation

The second stage develops an implementation and impact evaluation framework for the E-TOD proposal. It moves beyond policy design to consider how the proposal could be operationalised, monitored and evaluated over time.

Theory of Change

Establish the relationship between policy inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes and the intended long-term impact.

Logframe

Translate the policy objectives into a structured results framework with indicators, targets and means of verification.

Monitoring & Evaluation

Define key performance indicators and an evaluation approach to assess implementation progress and policy outcomes.
