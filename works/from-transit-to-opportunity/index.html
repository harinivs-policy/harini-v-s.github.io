From Transit to Opportunity

Employment-Oriented Transit Development (E-TOD)

A policy proposal that reframes public transport around a more fundamental question: not how much infrastructure a city builds, but how effectively that infrastructure connects people to work.

Policy Impact Challenge Series Challenge 6 + 7 Policy Design & Evaluation
01 / The policy problem

A city can build more transit and still leave workers disconnected.

India's expanding urban transport networks do not always keep pace with the geography of employment. Industrial clusters and job centres can lie beyond convenient metro and bus connections, leaving workers dependent on multiple transfers and costly last-mile journeys.

The challenge is not simply a shortage of transport infrastructure. It is a mismatch between where transport networks are developed, where employment opportunities are located, and where workers live.

When public transport does not connect residential areas with employment centres, workers may face longer journeys, higher commuting costs and reduced access to livelihood opportunities.

Poorly connected journeys can also increase dependence on private vehicles, adding to congestion and urban air pollution.

The proposed policy shift

Measure transport success by the jobs people can reach, not merely by the kilometres of track cities build.

02 / Challenge 6 · Policy design

From Development-Oriented Transit to Employment-Oriented Transit.

The policy brief proposes Employment-Oriented Transit Development (E-TOD): a framework that places employment access, affordability and universal mobility at the centre of urban transport planning.

06
Policy brief

From infrastructure expansion to job accessibility

The proposal argues that transit investment should be assessed by whether it improves workers' access to employment, particularly in industrial clusters, peripheral job centres and growing urban corridors.

It brings transport planning, land use, housing, affordability and employment geography into one policy framework.

What the policy proposes

01 · Public Jobs Access Score A city-level measure of how effectively residents can reach employment opportunities through public transport.
02 · Worker Transit Pass A proposed shared expenditure model involving employers, workers and government.
03 · Affordable housing near stations Mandatory affordable housing provisions around transit stations to support access and reduce spatial exclusion.
04 · Employment-oriented land use Encourage office clusters and employment-generating activities near transit, alongside housing and retail.
05 · Blended funding mechanisms Explore viability gap funding, value capture finance and co-funding arrangements.
06 · Mode neutrality Support buses, feeder services, e-autos and metro as parts of an integrated mobility system.
07 · Context-specific planning Differentiate strategies for established job centres, emerging corridors and smaller cities.
08 · Universal accessibility Incorporate accessibility needs across the complete public transport journey.

Core principle: Transit planning should be employment-sensitive, affordable, inclusive and responsive to the actual geography of work.

03 / Intended policy outcomes

What should a successful commute look like?

The policy brief articulates measurable aspirations for travel time and affordability. These are proposed targets, not claims of achieved outcomes.

45 min

Proposed target: help workers reach employment opportunities within 45 minutes.

10%

Proposed ceiling: keep daily commuting expenditure at or below 10% of a worker's daily wage.
