The Policy Debate / AI & Governance

Project DRISHTI

Examining the changing role of human judgement in AI-assisted freight wagon inspection across Indian Railways.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE RAILWAY GOVERNANCE HUMAN OVERSIGHT
The central question

Should AI decide, or only alert?

01 / The Context

When inspection becomes automated

Indian Railways' Project DRISHTI uses AI-enabled cameras to inspect moving freight wagons for issues such as open doors, broken locks and tampered seals.

The technology offers the possibility of faster and wider inspection coverage. AI can screen wagons as trains pass, potentially changing the scale and speed of freight inspection.

But expanding automated screening raises a larger governance question: when AI screens every wagon, what should remain the responsibility of human inspectors?

02 / The Debate

Three questions worth examining

The introduction of AI into safety-sensitive public systems is not merely a question of technological capability. It is also a question of authority, accountability and trust.

QUESTION 01

AI coverage versus human sampling

Is 100% AI screening with human spot-checks a more effective approach than 100% human screening of a smaller sample?

QUESTION 02

Can humans challenge an AI flag?

Can a junior inspector override an AI-generated alert? Who is accountable if the overridden decision subsequently proves incorrect?

QUESTION 03

What should people be trained for?

As AI enters railways, roads and courts, how should professional training evolve to prepare people for increasingly automated systems?

03 / Governance Perspective

Technology needs accountability

The debate is not simply about whether AI can detect an anomaly. It is about how automated alerts, human judgement and institutional responsibility should work together in safety-sensitive systems.

The central challenge is to establish a relationship between technological efficiency and meaningful human oversight. An automated system can identify a potential problem, but institutions must still define how alerts are reviewed, challenged, escalated and documented.

Important distinction: The governance approach explored in this project is a proposed analytical perspective for discussion. It should not be interpreted as an official policy, operating procedure or approved framework of Indian Railways or Project DRISHTI.
