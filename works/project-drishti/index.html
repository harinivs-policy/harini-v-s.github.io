The Policy Debate · AI & Governance

Project DRISHTI

Examining the changing role of human judgement in AI-assisted freight wagon inspection across Indian Railways.

The central question

Should AI decide, or only alert?

01 / The context

When inspection becomes automated

Indian Railways' Project DRISHTI uses AI-enabled cameras to inspect moving freight wagons for issues such as open doors, broken locks and tampered seals. The technology offers the possibility of faster and wider inspection coverage.

But expanding automated screening raises a governance question: when AI screens every wagon, what should remain the responsibility of human inspectors?

02 / The debate

Three questions worth examining

QUESTION 01

AI coverage versus human sampling

Is AI screening with human spot-checks preferable to human inspection of a smaller sample?

QUESTION 02

Can humans challenge an AI flag?

Can a junior inspector override an AI-generated alert? Who is accountable when an override leads to a wrong decision?

QUESTION 03

What should people be trained for?

As AI enters railways, roads and courts, how should professional training evolve?

03 / Governance perspective

Technology needs accountability

The debate is not simply about whether AI can detect an anomaly. It is about how automated alerts, human judgement and institutional responsibility should work together in safety-sensitive systems.

Important distinction: The governance approach explored in this project is a proposed analytical perspective for discussion. It should not be interpreted as an official policy or operating procedure of Indian Railways or Project DRISHTI.
